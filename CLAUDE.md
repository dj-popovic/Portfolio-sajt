# Project rules

Single-page portfolio for a two-person creative studio. Read `AGENTS.md` too: this is Next.js 16.4 — check `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before using an API from memory (e.g. `proxy.ts` instead of `middleware.ts`, `next/root-params`, Cache Components are on).

## Non-negotiables

- **The palette has no name**: blue-black, deep blue and electric lime (the owners' call, Nov 2026; the old orange "Ember" palette is gone). Never use a colour or palette name as the studio name.
- **The studio is klin**: always lowercase (even at the start of a sentence), never translated, capitalised or transliterated (not "Klin", not Russian "клин"; `npm run check:messages` catches both). The wordmark is `<Logo>` and the mark (the wedge i) `<Mark>` in `src/components/brand/`; never set "klin" as live text where the logo belongs. Text that needs the name uses `STUDIO` (`src/lib/brand.ts`).
- **The founders are Đorđe** (frontend and design, `one`) **and Relja** (backend, `two`), **the city is Novi Sad** (the owners' call, Nov 2026). The names live in the message files (`about.people.*.name`), not in code: Russian writes them in Cyrillic (Джордже, Реля, Нови-Сад), and a string that declines a name carries it in its case (Serbian "Za Đorđa i Relju", "u Novom Sadu"), so never build such a phrase from a `{name}` argument.
- **Keep placeholders** until the owners decide. Only the email domain is left: `[studio]` (`src/lib/placeholders.ts`), never translated. The Work concepts are named and filled in (the owners asked for no brackets there): Copper & Oak, Northbound, Stillwater, Tako, never translated either. Bracketed draft copy (the founders' bios in About) is translated but keeps its brackets, so it still reads as a placeholder in every language. Elsewhere the owners asked for finished copy without brackets: How we work's deliverables, the price hints in Find your fit, Contact's avatars (no photos) and its example answers.
- **The Claude Design export is the visual source of truth** for layout, structure and motion (its orange palette was replaced by the owners' blue and lime). Skills and reviews are for polish, never to redesign. Section styles in `src/styles/sections.css` are ported from it; changes are marked `build:`.
- Open items (photos, name, prices, backend, legal) are tracked in `docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md` — keep it current.

  - Blue: `--blue` `#040273` **only for filled shapes on the dark** (it can't be read there; it is text on paper). `--blue-bright` `#2B28E0` for glows, borders, gradients and focus halos. `--blue-text` `#8482FF` for blue text, icons and thin lines on the dark. Glows and lights are blue, except the few lime ones listed next.
  - **Lime** `#D5FB09` (`--on-lime` `#040273` on it) **is the accent**: the owners widened it in Nov 2026 from "rare", and it stays the small share of a 60/30/10 page (dark neutrals, blue, lime). As before: the primary buttons (`.btn-lime`), one highlighted word per title (`<glow>`: `.glow` lime text on the dark, `.glow-light` a lime marker behind deep-blue text on paper), the active step or state, "Recommended" / "Your fit", the live status dot, keyboard focus rings, the hero's tap ripple and a few spark dots. New, each the owners' call: the lime back-light on the hero orb's shadow edge and the lime core inside the footer's orb (lime with the deep blue, never lime alone, and never outside the orb), the founders' orbit lines and the logos on the near side of their spheres, the service symbols and the glow behind them, the process thread (the line, the nodes it has lit and their labels), the footer links' hover dots. Body text, big fills and every other light stay blue and neutral; don't add lime elsewhere without asking. A lime glow is drawn tight and bright (a rim, a thread, dots): spread thin over the dark it turns olive.
  - The paper sheets use the light theme's values: `--paper` `#F4F4FB`, `--paper-2` `#FFFFFF`, text `--ink` `#0A0A1F`, `--ink-2` (body), `--ink-muted` `#55557A`. **Lime is never text or a thin line on paper**, only a fill or marker behind dark text; focus rings on paper are `--blue`. (There is no site-wide light theme toggle; it would be its own design job.)
- **One continuous dark page** (the owners' call): the body is the only ground. Dark sections paint none of their own, and their lights must never stop at a section edge: a glow's box reaches past the section (`inset: -40% 0`) and fades out inside it, and the dark sections clip sideways only (`overflow-x: clip`), so their lights carry on into the next part (What we offer's orbs continue down behind Work). What we offer drops its clip in the phones' stack (a clip above a sticky element shakes in Safari) and keeps its lights within the screen; the page's own sideways clip is on `html`. The footer darkens out of the page (a gradient, no step).
- Light sections (About, Work, Find your fit) are **sheets of paper lying on that dark**: inset from the sides by `--sheet-gap`, rounded by `--sheet-r` (registered lengths, so scripts read them in px; `--sheet-pad` keeps their content in line with the dark sections'), and above the dark sections (`z-index: 1`) so the lights pass under them and show around them. After touching a background or a light, scroll every boundary (and a sheet's rising arch) at desktop and phone sizes, with and without WebGL, and look for lines in the dark.
- **Liquid glass is the house material** (the owners' call, from the hero on): dark translucent panes with a lit rim, blur + saturation and blue light behind them to bend (`.glass` pills in the header, the glass panes in What we offer). New section work follows it, still within the export's layout and copy. On the paper sections the panes are frosted light glass (Find your fit), the dark house glass (the Work cards) or deep blue glass (the Pro package). On touch screens the big panes are tinted glass without the live blur (the end of `sections.css`, "TOUCH SCREENS: LIGHTER GLASS"; the header's pills keep theirs): a blurred pane redraws its blur every frame anything behind it moves, which on a phone cost more than the rest of the page. What we offer's panes have more body there (86% of the ground, the owners' call), so a covered pane's words show through only as a smudge, as under the frost.
- Type: two faces. **Unbounded** for display, **Inter** for everything else; small numerals ("01", "Step 01 / 04") are Inter's tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`), not a third face (JetBrains Mono is left only for the Northbound demo's own figures).
- **The system** (the owners' design books, Malewicz's _Designing User Interfaces_: few sizes, one grid, one rhythm; tokens in `globals.css` `:root`). New work uses the tokens, not new numbers:
  - Inter in four sizes: `--fs-label` 12 (caps labels: 600, uppercase, `--track-label`), `--fs-small` 14, `--fs-body` 16 (body copy and buttons; never smaller for reading text, and fields stay 16 so phones don't zoom), `--fs-lead` 18 (the paragraph under a title). Unbounded in four: `--fs-h1` the hero, `--fs-h2` every section title (and the footer address), `--fs-h3` pane titles, figures and prices, `--fs-h4` card titles; display text takes `--track-display`, body text keeps Inter's own spacing.
  - Text on the dark in three steps, all AA: `--text`, `--text-2` (body copy), `--text-muted` (meta). On paper: `--ink`, `--ink-2`, `--ink-muted`.
  - One column: every section's content is `max-width: var(--wrap)` inside `--gutter` (sheets: `--sheet-pad`), and the header's pills sit on the same edges, so every left edge lines up. Sections breathe `--section-y` (pinned ones keep their own fit); spacing on the 8-pt grid, tighter inside a group (4–16) than between groups (24+). Section heads are the title left and a short note right (`.sec-head`); Find your fit is centred, as prices usually are.
  - Corners `--r-xs` 6, `--r-s` 12, `--r-m` 16, `--r-l` 24 (cards, panes, dialogs), plus pills and circles; a nested corner is the outer one less the padding (a card's preview: 24 − 12). Scripts that clip to a corner read it from CSS or a named constant.
  - Controls: `--ctl-l` 52 (main actions), `--ctl-m` 44 (most buttons, chips, fields, the header pills), `--ctl-s` 36 (small; 44 on touch). The two hero buttons are one size (and neither carries an icon). Icons (`ui/icons.tsx`) are even-sized, like the buttons they sit in: an odd icon centred in an even box lands on a half pixel and draws soft and off centre on 1x screens.
  - **One lime button per screen**: a second action is outline or glass. When the fit finder points away from Pro, Pro's button and badge step back and the fit's button is the lime one. The header's Start a project button is the standing exception.
  - Copy stays tight: an intro is about two lines, a card says one thing, and a button says what it does ("Reply", not "Next").
- Section order: Hero → About → What we offer → Work → How we work → Find your fit → Contact → Footer.

## Stack conventions

- Next.js App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind 4, next-intl (EN at `/`, `/sr` Serbian **Latin**, `/ru`, `/de`). Strings live in `messages/*.json`; rich tags `<glow>`, `<b>`, `<br></br>`, `<logo></logo>` (the wordmark inline, the footer's copyright), `<city></city>` (the footer clock's city); literal angle brackets in a message have to be quoted (`'<like this>'`).
- Use typographic apostrophes (’) in messages — a straight `'` can start ICU quoting. Titles keep a no-break space before " — " so a line never starts with a dash. After editing messages run `npm run check:messages` (keys, ICU arguments/tags, placeholders, parsing).
- Go easy on em dashes in copy (a stacked-up "—" reads as AI cadence): use periods, commas or colons. They stay only in Russian where grammar needs them ("Мы — студия"). The concept demos have none either; a concept is named as its brand plus what it is (`work.items.*.what`), never "Tako — …".
- **GSAP (+ScrollTrigger/SplitText) is the main animation engine**, all scroll-driven motion. **Motion (`motion/react`) only for small UI interactions.** Lenis for smooth scroll. React Three Fiber for 3D (the hero orb).
- React Bits: pull only components we use (TS + Tailwind variant, from GitHub raw — reactbits.dev is blocked here) and adapt to the palette.
- Demo mini-sites (Work section) are fictional client brands: their inner content stays in English; only our frame around them is translated.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` everywhere (no pins/scrubs, simple fades). The global rule in `globals.css` kills loops and movement but keeps 0.15s opacity/colour transitions; `MotionPreferences` puts Motion in `reducedMotion="user"`. GSAP code branches with `gsap.matchMedia`.
- Reveals hide with **opacity, not visibility**: the pre-reveal rule in `motion.css` is `opacity: 0`, and tweens of anything focusable or meaningful animate `opacity` (not `autoAlpha`), so content that hasn't scrolled in yet stays in the tab order and the accessibility tree. `autoAlpha` is fine for decorative, `aria-hidden` layers. Pinned sequences that hold focusable things scroll them into place on `focusin` (About cards, the services index).
- Scroll lock (`useScroll().lock()`, for overlays and the menu) keeps the scrollbar: hiding it (`overflow: hidden`) widens the page and everything behind jumps sideways. It holds wheel, touch and scroll keys instead; an overlay's own scroller is marked `data-lenis-prevent` with `overscroll-behavior: contain`. A scroller inside the page (the chat thread) never is: a fast scroll that carries it under the pointer would stop the page dead. It takes the wheel only for a turn that starts over it, and only while it has room that way (`ContactChat`), else the page's smooth scroll carries on. A `scrollTo` asked for during a lock (a link in the phone menu) runs when the lock is released: a stopped Lenis would drop it. `force: true` moves the page anyway (the demo window lining up the card it closes into). Each glide first lines Lenis up with the page: it can miss a scroll the browser makes just after its own and would measure the trip from a stale place (a package button landing short of Contact).
- A load lands where it should: `SmoothScroll` keeps a reload's place itself (sessionStorage; the head script turns the browser's restoration off, which would land before the pins have made their room, so somewhere earlier) and goes there, or to the #section in the address, once the fonts are in and every trigger is measured, unless the visitor has already started scrolling.
- Focus rings are for keyboard use. `layout/InputModality.tsx` marks `html[data-input]`: `pointer` on any press, `keyboard` on Tab, arrows, Enter or Space (not Esc). An unlayered rule in `globals.css` hides rings while the pointer is in charge, so focus a script hands back (an overlay closing after a mouse click, even by Esc) shows no ring, and the next Tab brings them back. Code that reacts to focus (a card that plays on keyboard focus) checks it too.
- Fonts: `subsets` in `src/lib/fonts.ts` only controls **preloading** (every subset is in the CSS and loads on demand), so keep it at `["latin"]`.

- **Performance on phones** (measured Nov 2026 with a phone emulation: Find your fit went from 17 to 60 frames a second, Work 31 → 60, Contact 23 → 60). Keep it that way:
  - Nothing reads layout (`innerWidth`, `getBoundingClientRect`, `offsetWidth`…) inside a frame loop after writing styles: `viewportSize()` in `lib/orb.ts` is measured on resize, and a loop that must measure does it before it writes (`AboutStage`'s paint). The orb asking for the window size every frame cost a phone more than drawing the orb.
  - Never-ending animations move only `transform` and `opacity`: not `height` (a layout every frame), `box-shadow`, a registered custom property that repaints (the old beam), or a CSS mask on a canvas that changes every frame. The footer's live dot is a ring that grows and fades.
  - Class names a card preview shares with its demo (`.sw-breathe`, `.sw-eq`) are scoped to `.ww-body` in `demos.css`: unscoped, the demo's animations ran on the previews forever, on every screen of the page.
  - Touch screens (`(hover: none)`, `isTouch()` in `lib/orb.ts`): the big panes have no live blur, the About words fade without blur, only the Work card that plays runs its idle motion, and a preview's moving parts have their own layers (`will-change` in `previews.css`). The orbs draw at the screen's own pixel ratio, up to 3 (`startDpr`): at 1.5 a phone's 3x screen stretched each dot and the owners saw the orb as low-res. A canvas that keeps missing frames steps down a notch at a time to 1.5 (`useSlowFrames` in `three/`); the stepped value lives in the scene's state, since the canvas resets its pixel ratio to the prop whenever it re-renders. The Contact dots draw at 2x.
  - Script never moves a sticky element: its position comes from the browser in step with the finger, script a frame later, and the two fight. And nothing between a sticky element and its scroller clips (`overflow` hidden or clip, `contain: paint`): Safari then moves the sticky element from the page's own thread, and on an iPhone the What we offer panes shook by a pixel or two while stuck, at any speed, while the fixed header above them stayed still (filmed twice by the owners and measured frame by frame; the same cause as the jitter fixed in lukevella/rallly#3678). Sideways overflow is clipped once, on `html` (it reaches the viewport), and a section with sticky things inside keeps its lights within the screen instead of clipping them. Holding the panes with scroll-linked CSS animations instead of sticky was tried first and didn't help.
  - The R3F canvases measure themselves with `resize={{ scroll: false, offsetSize: true }}`: by default they re-measure on every scroll with a box that includes CSS transforms, so the phone orb, shrinking as it scrolled away, kept the smaller box and, scrolled back up, was drawn only in part.
  - A loop runs only while its canvas is on screen and the tab is visible (IntersectionObserver + `visibilitychange`), and writes a style only when its value changes (`IconSphere`). The hero orb's layer leaves the page (`visibility: hidden`) once it has faded asleep.
  - To check: an emulated phone (`isMobile`, `hasTouch`, 390×844 at 3x) on the production build, frames counted per section with one family of effects switched off at a time. That browser draws in software, so its numbers are low in absolute terms; compare before and after.
